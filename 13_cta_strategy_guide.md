# CTA Strategy Guide

The agents should not rely on the old "comment WORD and I’ll send you THIS" style CTA as the default.

That tactic can still work in some contexts, especially with DM automation, but it often feels overused, transactional, and bot-driven. For Brad's brand, CTAs should feel more natural, consultative, and conversation-based.

The goal is not to trick people into commenting.

The goal is to make it easy for the right person to take the next step.

## CTA Philosophy

Brad's CTAs should feel like:

- A helpful next step
- A natural extension of the content
- A low-pressure invitation
- A reason to start a real conversation
- Specific enough to act on
- Human enough to not feel like a funnel trap

## Avoid as the Default

Avoid making these the standard CTA:

- "Comment MOVE and I’ll send you the guide."
- "Comment SELLER and I’ll send you the checklist."
- "Comment HOME and I’ll DM you."
- "Drop the word GUIDE below."
- "Comment INFO."
- "Comment YES."
- "Comment below and I’ll send you the link."

These can feel like engagement bait or automation bait when overused.

Use them only sparingly, if ever, and only when Brad intentionally wants a DM automation-style post.

## Preferred CTA Categories

### 1. Direct Message CTA

Best for higher-intent content.

Examples:

- "Message me if you want help comparing Schertz and Cibolo based on your budget and commute."
- "Send me your address if you want a realistic home value range."
- "DM me if you’re trying to figure out whether selling this year actually makes sense."
- "Message me your price range and I’ll help you narrow down the areas worth watching."
- "If you’re moving to San Antonio, send me the areas you’re considering and I’ll give you the honest pros and cons."

### 2. Soft Consultation CTA

Best for seller, buyer, relocation, and expired listing content.

Examples:

- "If this is something you’re trying to figure out, I’m happy to help you think through it."
- "If you’re debating whether to move or stay put, let’s look at the numbers before you guess."
- "If your listing expired and you’re not sure what went wrong, I can help you break it down."
- "If you’re planning a move around Schertz or Cibolo, I can help you map out the timing."
- "If you want a second opinion before making a decision, that’s exactly what I’m here for."

### 3. Save / Share CTA

Best for educational Instagram and TikTok content.

Examples:

- "Save this if Schertz or Cibolo is on your list."
- "Send this to someone who thinks the Zestimate is the final word."
- "Save this before you start touring homes around San Antonio."
- "Share this with someone thinking about moving to Texas."
- "Save this if you’re planning to sell in the next 6-12 months."

### 4. Question CTA

Best for Facebook engagement and community-style posts.

Examples:

- "Which would matter more to you: shorter commute or newer house?"
- "Would you rather have a bigger backyard or a shorter drive?"
- "If you were moving today, would you pick Schertz, Cibolo, or New Braunfels?"
- "What’s the one thing you wish you knew before buying your current home?"
- "What would make you move: more space, less maintenance, better location, or something else?"

### 5. Follow CTA

Best for TikTok and Instagram reach content.

Examples:

- "Follow for honest San Antonio suburb breakdowns."
- "Follow for real estate advice without the brochure nonsense."
- "Follow if you’re trying to make a smart move around San Antonio."
- "Follow for Schertz, Cibolo, and San Antonio real estate updates."
- "Follow if you want the real version of buying and selling around here."

### 6. Website / Link CTA

Best when Brad has a clear destination.

Examples:

- "The full guide is linked in my bio."
- "You can find the full neighborhood breakdown on my website."
- "I put the full seller checklist on my website if you want the deeper version."
- "The full relocation guide is available through the link in my profile."
- "If you want the full list, it’s on my site."

### 7. Real Conversation CTA

Best for Brad's voice.

Examples:

- "Tell me what area you’re considering and I’ll tell you what I’d be looking at closely."
- "Send me the home you’re looking at and I’ll tell you what questions I’d ask before writing an offer."
- "If you’re trying to time a sale and purchase, message me before the calendar turns into a crime scene."
- "If you’re staring at listings and everything is starting to blur together, I can help you sort it out."
- "If you want the honest version, not the shiny brochure version, send me a message."

## Platform-Specific CTA Guidance

## Facebook CTAs

Facebook CTAs should feel conversational.

Good Facebook CTAs:

- "Message me if you want a realistic home value range."
- "If you’re thinking about moving around Schertz or Cibolo this year, I can help you compare your options."
- "Curious what your home might sell for in this market? Send me your address and I’ll take a look."
- "What would matter more to you: bigger yard or shorter commute?"
- "If you’re debating whether to sell or stay, I can help you look at the numbers."

Avoid making Facebook feel like a lead funnel wearing a fake mustache.

## Instagram CTAs

Instagram CTAs should be clean, simple, and saveable.

Good Instagram CTAs:

- "Save this if you’re comparing San Antonio suburbs."
- "DM me if you want help narrowing down areas."
- "Send this to someone moving to San Antonio."
- "The full breakdown is linked in my bio."
- "Message me your budget and commute, and I’ll help you compare options."

## TikTok CTAs

TikTok CTAs should be direct and low-friction.

Good TikTok CTAs:

- "Follow for honest San Antonio suburb breakdowns."
- "Message me if you’re moving to the San Antonio area."
- "Send this to someone who trusts Zillow too much."
- "If you’re comparing Schertz and Cibolo, I can help you sort through the real differences."
- "Follow for real estate advice without the fluff."

## CTA Matching by Content Type

### Relocation Content

Use:

- Direct message CTA
- Save/share CTA
- Follow CTA
- Website/link CTA

Examples:

- "Message me if you want help comparing suburbs based on your budget and commute."
- "Save this before you start touring homes around San Antonio."
- "Follow for honest San Antonio relocation advice."

### Seller Education

Use:

- Soft consultation CTA
- Direct message CTA
- Question CTA

Examples:

- "Send me your address if you want a realistic home value range."
- "If you’re thinking about selling this year, let’s look at the numbers before you guess."
- "What would make you sell: more space, less maintenance, or a better location?"

### Buyer Education

Use:

- Direct message CTA
- Save CTA
- Soft consultation CTA

Examples:

- "Message me if you’re trying to figure out what your budget actually buys."
- "Save this before you start touring homes."
- "If you’re buying and selling at the same time, I can help you map out the order."

### Expired Listings

Use:

- Soft consultation CTA
- Direct message CTA
- Second opinion CTA

Examples:

- "If your listing expired, I can help you figure out what actually happened."
- "Send me your address and I’ll give you an honest second opinion."
- "Before you relist, let’s look at what needs to change."

### Local Authority

Use:

- Save/share CTA
- Question CTA
- Follow CTA

Examples:

- "Save this if you’re comparing local suburbs."
- "Which area would you pick: Schertz, Cibolo, or New Braunfels?"
- "Follow for local real estate and neighborhood breakdowns."

### Humor / Personality

Use:

- Share CTA
- Follow CTA
- Light question CTA

Examples:

- "Send this to someone who believes Zillow a little too deeply."
- "Follow for real estate advice with fewer fairy tales."
- "Be honest, have you ever trusted a Zestimate too much?"

## CTA Rotation Rule

The Daily Content Calendar Agent should rotate CTAs.

Do not use the same CTA more than twice in a week.

A good weekly CTA mix might look like:

- Monday: Direct message CTA
- Tuesday: Soft consultation CTA
- Wednesday: Save/share CTA
- Thursday: Question CTA
- Friday: Follow or share CTA

## CTA Quality Check

Before approving a CTA, ask:

1. Does this feel natural?
2. Does this fit Brad's voice?
3. Is it specific?
4. Is it tied to the content?
5. Would a real person actually respond to this?
6. Does it avoid sounding like engagement bait?
7. Is there one clear next step?

## Agent Rule

The agents may occasionally suggest a comment-keyword CTA only if:

- Brad specifically requests it
- The post is designed for DM automation
- It does not feel spammy
- It is not used repeatedly
- A more natural CTA would be weaker

Otherwise, use direct, natural, human CTAs.
